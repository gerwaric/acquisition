# 30 — The review: brief for the seat in stage 4 of the synthesis

Two designers, working apart, each designed an item search for
Acquisition: the model a person at a terminal and an agent over MCP
will both think in. You are about to be their first user. You will use
both designs, one after the other, and say what it was like.

Each design has already been checked against its page and its
citations, and repaired by its author. That work is not yours, you will
never see it, and you need not repeat it. Yours is the thing no check
can collect: what it costs to understand each design, what it is like
to ask real questions in it, and what in each should survive whatever
else happens. You are not here to pick a winner for the owner, to merge
the two, or to design a third. You are here to have used them,
honestly.

This file is committed before either run, deleted when the stage
closes, and cited by hash.

## What you read

1. This brief.
2. `search/DIGEST.md` — the evidence; its acceptance set holds the
   questions a human and an agent really asked of a real stash.
3. `brainstorming-notes/22-search-framing.md` — the goal, the owner's
   stances, the settled floor.
4. `brainstorming-notes/29-search-proposal-brief.md` — what the
   designers were asked, so you know what they were reaching for.
5. The two designs, in the order given to you:
   - Fable: `search/designs/design-a.md`, then `design-b.md`.
   - Astra: `search/designs/design-b.md`, then `design-a.md`.

The repository's standing reading (`AGENTS.md`, `README.md`,
`CONTEXT.md`) applies. **Never:** anything under
`search/proposal-audit/`; `brainstorming-notes/23-*`, `24-*`, `25-*`,
`32-*` or any other note; the index
`search/README.md`; the other review; the code; anything you remember
from another session, and if you are Fable, the memory files on item
search. Do not try to work out who wrote which design. It cannot help
you, and the attempt will colour what you feel. Nothing here needs the
daemon, the store or the network; do not run them.

## How to use a design

Do all of this for the first design before you open the second.

- **Read it whole, without a pen.** Then say what you take this design
  to *be* — its one idea — in as few lines as that honestly takes, and
  count the lines. This is your reading, not a neutral summary.
- **Then use it.** Think of the stash as yours: tens of thousands of
  items, years of them. Write at least five questions you would
  actually want to ask of it — not the designers' examples, yours — at
  least one where one or more precise requirements sit among broader
  ones (note 22, stance 3). Add two from the digest's acceptance set.
  Ask each in the design's notation, using what the design gives you
  and nothing else. Then walk in as a stranger: tool descriptions and
  help only, no row seen — how do you get from nothing to your first
  correct answer?
- **Keep notes as you go, as the user you are.** Where the design
  fought you. What you reached for that was not there. What you got
  wrong on the first try, and whether the design would have told you.
  What you enjoyed. First person, each note tied to the question you
  were asking when you felt it.
- **Say what it is again.** After using it, write the one idea a second
  time. What changed, and why? What it cost you to understand this
  design is something nobody else can measure.

Then the second design, the same way. You will meet it tired, and with
the first in your head: notice where that helps it and where it hurts
it, and say so.

## Then, having used both

- **Which would you choose to work in**, tomorrow, with a real task and
  a budget of tokens and patience? Why? Would your answer change if you
  were a person at a terminal instead of an agent? If you were a
  different agent, one that thinks less like you?
- **What should survive from each**, stated at its strongest — the idea
  you would be sorry to lose, even from the design you did not choose.
- **Two steps out.** A GUI that must answer in a keystroke; PoE2 in
  December; an interface nobody has named yet; the owner a year from
  now with none of this in mind. Where does each design bend, and where
  does it break?
- **What they made you think of.** At most three ideas that using these
  designs sparked, a few lines each, as possibilities. Never a
  replacement design: the two in front of you are what is being judged.

## What you hand back

One file — Fable: `brainstorming-notes/26-search-review-by-fable.md`;
Astra: `brainstorming-notes/27-search-review-by-astra.md` — in this
order:

1. One line of provenance: who you are, the order you read in, and the
   commit of this brief.
2. For each design, in the order you used it: the one idea before use
   and its line count; your questions in its notation, with what
   happened; the cold start as you walked it; your notes; the one idea
   after use, and what changed.
3. Having used both: your choice and why; what should survive from
   each; two steps out; what they made you think of.
4. What you left out, and the one cut you would most want reversed.

About 12 KB is a guide, never a target.

Testimony is owned, not proved. It needs no citation; it needs to be
true. Where you state a fact about a design — that it cannot ask
something, that a construct is undefined — quote the line, so nobody
mistakes your experience for a measurement or a measurement for your
experience. Do not be kind to be fair and do not be harsh to seem
rigorous. A user who liked nothing and a user who liked everything have
both told the owner nothing.

Commit your one file yourself, with a message that says which design
you would work in and what you are least sure of. Touch nothing else.
Do not push.
