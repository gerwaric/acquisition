## 7. The one page

Measured: §1 is 57 lines / 536 words / 3,031 bytes; §2 is 29 lines / 281
words / 1,817 bytes; together 87 lines / 817 words / 4,849 bytes, a third
of the proposal's 14,607. Note 22 sets no measure for "one page", so the
count is the result: the model and its grammar are 87 lines, more than
one 24-line screen and about one and a half printed pages at 500 words.

Could a second implementer build the same search from those 87 lines
alone? No. What they would have to fetch from elsewhere in the design, or
from nowhere:

- The rest of the field list — §1 prints ~27 of "about thirty" and ends
  in `…`; each field's derivation is nowhere (F9).
- The shipped totals, their names and their definitions (§3), which two
  acceptance queries use (F8).
- `class`'s vocabulary and its base → class table, and `class:unknown`
  (§3).
- The kind vocabulary for `kind:`/`has:`, and the flag vocabulary for
  `is:` — §2 says only "`is:` takes the flags"; discovery is §4.3.
- Productions for `has:` and `is:` (F5), the empty query (F6), a sort
  key's direction (F23), and a bare word's name-vs-text order (F27).
- The `--count` argument's forms: one name, `line`, `line <text>` (§1,
  §4.3), a comma list (AQ1 only, F6).
- `show`, its id forms, and `--against` (§4.6, AQ3; F18).
- `next`'s continuation argument — named in §1's block, defined nowhere.
- The projection the engine reads, the revision compare and the re-derive
  rule (§3), and the trade correspondence (§4.5).
- The canonicaliser, beyond §1's one sentence: number-to-`#`, the markup
  half, the sign, and `*`'s interaction with it (§2).
