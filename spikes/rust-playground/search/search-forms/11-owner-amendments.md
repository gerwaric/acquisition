# 11 — The owner's amendments to the consolidated page (2026-09-19)

After reading file 10 the owner asked four questions "around the edges"; the exchange produced nine changes, two open items and two things left as they were. File 10 carries the changes; this file records what was decided and why, with his words.

## Changed

1. **`pseudo.<name>` — one namespace for every computed value.** Named totals and derived fields both: whatever `--describe` prints a definition for (`pseudo.total-res`, `pseudo.dps`, `pseudo.pdps`, `pseudo.defence-pct`). Dotted, because `:` already has one meaning, contains. A ranged computed value takes its slot word last: `pseudo.cold-damage.avg>=30`. It does not promise the trade site's pseudo of a similar name. Owner: "i agree with 'pseudo' as the namespace now, which means we can fit stuff like the various kinds of dps and other calculated items. This doesn't need to 100% align with trade--the c++ never even did that, and the c++ went without any pseudomod calculations for years." He had first declined `total`: "not all of the trade pseudo mods are straight total."
   *Why a namespace and not a kind of line* — his other idea, pseudo as a source or flag inside `line(…)`. The trade site can fold pseudos in beside explicit stats because it has no occurrences: every filter is one number per item. Here C92 separates one occurrence from the item's total, and the two behave oppositely where it is hardest: a line the item lacks is false, a sum of nothing is zero; a readable line is a witness when another array is unread, a total with an unread contributor is incomplete; conditions bind to one occurrence, a total has none. The reuse he wanted exists at the value level — the same `.<slot>` reads a line and a ranged computed value.
2. **Discovery lists computed values.** A text-narrowed vocabulary read (`--count line:resist`) also returns the computed values whose name or definition matches, marked computed. Before this a total could be found only through `--describe`. Owner: "agreed and we will see what this does to implememtation, but it's always easier to cut stuff out than add it in".
3. **`~`, a pattern match, as the third text strength** beside `:` contains and `=` whole value, on every text thing, the template inside `line(…)` included; `text` becomes the explicit name of what a bare word searches (`text~"…"`). Rust regex syntax, unanchored, any case by default; a bad pattern is an authoring error; like `:` it is a continuing search whose resolution the answer lists and never freezes; templates with different slot shapes under one comparison are C92's existing error; no trade equivalent, so the translation's remainder. Two notes for the help: patterns for words, comparisons for numbers; a `sum` over pattern-selected lines is the user's arithmetic, not a reviewed total (a line for all elemental resistances counts once there, three times in `pseudo.total-res`). The cost over all displayed text is unmeasured. Owner: "Using ~ for regex makes sense"; "agreed."
4. **The projection is written `.<slot>`.** File 10 had `.slot`, which read as a literal. Instances: `line(P).high`, `line(P).avg`, `line(P).arg1`. And one meaning is now stated: `line(P).avg>=20` lowers to `line(P avg>=20)`. Owner: "I thought it was literally '.slot', when i should have read it as .<slot>, so to speak."

5. **What `text` searches, stated exactly.** File 10 said "everything the item displays", which was loose. Now: each displayed string on its own, never across two — the name, the base, every line as shown with its numbers (properties, requirements, mod lines), markup reduced to what the player sees; `template:` by contrast sees only the `#`-form and never a number. Not searched: flavour text, description text, and the note, which is its own field — a search for `life` should not hit a unique's poem; any of them can get its own named thing if a question wants it. A socketed gem is its own item (the ingest columns carry `socketed_in`), found by its own text with its place showing the parent. In a pattern `^` and `$` are the ends of one string. The row shows which string matched; no hit while part of the item is unread is undecided. Owner: "agreed".
6. **Positional slots are `arg1`, `arg2`, … — the owner's idea.** He asked whether `#1`, `#2` could lose the special character ("what about 'first' and 'second' for #1 and #2? That takes more tokens, but uses less special characters"), then, after a survey of other idioms (`$1`, `[1]`, `{1}`, `\1`, `%1`, Rust's `.1`, note 25's `n1`), proposed ".arg<N>" or ".arg(N)". `arg<N>` is the one applied: the template is a format string and its numbers are the arguments filled into it — Qt's `%1`/`.arg()` and every printf — so the word says what the thing is, where `n1` was only a letter; it is a plain word like `low`, `high` and `avg`, so every slot word is a word and `#` means one thing, the placeholder inside a quoted template; it survives shells, JSON, GitHub's `#1` issue links and a `~` pattern beside it; it scales to the census's seven-number template; it counts from 1. `.arg(N)` was set aside (Fable's choice between his two forms, his to reverse): inside the group it reads `line("T" arg(1)>=10 arg(2)<=5)`, it adds a parenthesised form whose content is a literal and never a query, and it suggests N could be an expression.
7. **Names use underscores, never hyphens:** `pseudo.total_res`, `pseudo.cold_damage`, `pseudo.defence_pct`. Owner: "for named pseudo-mods, let's use underscored, because dashes have meaning elsewhere." So `-` only ever means "not" or a minus sign. With it, his agreement that the language must be shown to be formally constructible as specified: a parser and printer with a round-trip test (C104's text ⇄ tree) is the first thing built.
8. **No `has:` on a computed value.** `has:pseudo.total_res` had meant "the value is established", true for an item with no resistances at all, which reads as its opposite. "Has some" is `pseudo.total_res>0`; "could not be worked out" is `undecided(pseudo.total_res)`; every count route is writable without it. It is an authoring error offering those two. The sum-status table in file 10 loses the column. Owner: "agree, no has:pseudo".
9. **A bare word is an authoring error that shows its readings; a quoted phrase is text.** `rare ring life` is refused with `rare → rarity=rare · "rare"`, `ring → class:ring · "ring"`, `life → "life" · line(template:life)`. This replaces what Astra and Fable had agreed (a bare word is always text) and keeps its reason: a field added later can never break an old bare-word search, because there are none. What it removes is the silent fall-through that rule cost — a mistyped term (`rarity rare`, `corrupted`) or the natural `rare ring` became a text search that returned a plausible, subtly wrong answer or an unexplained zero. It is C91's own rule (an ambiguity is an error that shows the readings), and it passes the owner's break-later test in the right direction: allowing bare words later breaks nothing, forbidding them later would. The stash-box habit is an adapter's job — a plain text box that wraps its input in `text:` — never the filter's. Owner: "I approve your proposed rule on bare words."

## Open, by the owner's word

- **The definition of a ranged line — a side quest for another session.** The contract says a ranged line is one whose template reads `# to #`. The census has templates it does not settle. Owner, 2026-09-19: on `# to # Added Physical Damage per # Armour or Evasion Rating on Shield`, "average is of the first two numbers"; on `Bow: Adds (#-#) to (#-#) Cold Damage`, "real items will only ever have 'Adds # to # Cold Damager'. The ranges-of-range are the underlying mods that allow the upper and lower limits to be randomized, but they will not appear in real items." Measured since: every `(#-#)` template in the census sits in `explicitMods` of items that *describe* a mod per slot (`Belt: Reflects (51-100) Physical Damage to Melee Attackers`, `Armour: (6-11)% to Cold Resistance`; 26 items carry the commonest) — consistent with his reading: they are descriptions of possible rolls, not rolled values, so `low`/`high`/`avg` should not apply to them, though they are lines and searchable. To settle: whether a `# to #` pair anywhere in a template takes the three words with the other numbers positional (his first answer), and what a template with two pairs does.
- **League as a default scope, and the cost of a terminal call.** Both are expected frictions, neither a language change: league is an adapter-level default like the parked sticky realm, its trigger the owner's first seat forgetting it; the per-call corpus load is the first measurement the build owes (the persistence park's 500 ms trigger). Owner: "i agree with your concerns, but i'm also not sure at what point we should think about addressing them." Answer recorded: at the harvest, one line added to the sticky-default park; then the triggers decide.

## Left as it was

- **`line(…)` keeps its name**, over `stat(…)` (the trade site's word, which brings the site's semantics: ids two thirds of lines have, the average on a ranged stat, local against global twins) and `mod(…)` (the players' and the C++ app's word; properties and requirements are lines and no vocabulary calls them mods, and the parked attribution feature is where the precise sense of "mod" belongs). The word tells the user what the search sees: what the item displays. Owner: "I agree with line()".
- **No suggestion pointing `mod(` or `stat(` at `line(…)`.** Owner: "dont worry about suggesting line() if someone tries mod() or stat() in error". They are ordinary unknown names.
