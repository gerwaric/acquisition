## What is built

Category is computed from the item's class and reviewed rules in one
place — `category.rs`, which the paragraph names — and never by a
second classifier (the plan, rule 10). One question is asked of an
item there — does this id hold: yes, no, or undecided with its reason
— and the term, the count and the help all ask it. Piece C will ask it
too, of `weapon`, from the totals; leave the door inside the crate and
build nothing of C.

The meaning is the paragraph's and J1–J4, J9–J22: the 82 ids of the
site's filter as spelled; a parent, its own classes and everything
under it by the dot; a grouping, its reviewed members alone; a leaf, a
class's, or a class's with a rule on the base name; a beast by its
bestiary line, its colour the colour table's row; what a count lists
an item under; what is undecided and what is known absence.

What it rests on enters as reference data under C106's admission
test: generated by a committed script from pinned, committed sources —
the site's filter of 2026-09-12, the class table, the track's
`grouping-members.csv` and `beast-colours.csv` — each row citing what
says so, checked by the script's `--check` as `tools/class-table.py`
checks its own, its version on every answer's basis. The generator
does not import `expected.py`, and no test's expectation is read off
the build: the table is the tests' side, the sources and the rulings
are the build's.

The class changes with it, in `class.rs` and nowhere else: a beast's
class is `Stackable Currency`, read by its bestiary line (J9); Growing
Wombgift's is `Wombgifts` (J20). The class table stays what the export
states: a departure a ruling makes is a named rule citing the ruling,
never an unmarked row. The category is keyed by the class as
`class.rs` names it; if two of the export's classes share a displayed
name and the site puts them under different ids, that is a stop.

## The work, in order

1. **The tests, before any code, each seen to fail for the reason you
   expect.**
   - *The table.* One test reads `expected-answers.csv` where it lies,
     unchanged, and for each of its 550 rows puts an item of those
     facts — realm, base, frame, the bestiary line — into a fixture
     store through the store's own ingest. Every one of the 82 ids is
     then asked as a term: the items that match are the rows listing
     the id under `ids_yes`, the items undecided on it the rows
     listing it under `ids_undecided`, and every other row fails it.
     The count by category lists each row under `listed_under`. The
     class is `class`, or not established where
     `class_not_established` says why. A cause pins what could not be
     established — the class, by the gap `class.rs` names, or the
     colour — and never a wording; the table's two causes for a base
     with no class (the table lacks it; the export prints no name for
     its class) are one gap in this build, the one `class.rs` has.
     The `site_*` and `agrees_*` columns are evidence and assert
     nothing.
   - *OQ5 by category*, beside the test of OQ5 in
     `tests/acceptance.rs`, over the same fixture: the wearable
     classes spelled as `(category=armour or category=accessory)`, the
     level and the lines as they stand. It answers `boots` and `ring`
     with one undecided, as the class spelling does, and the test's
     doc says the ids are the fixture's and claim no more (the owner,
     2026-10-06: "It can include flasks and even cluster jewels or
     abyssal jewels depending on the context."). The class-spelled
     test stays.
   - *A beast found by its category*: a fixture you work by hand
     before running — a yellow beast, a red one, one the colour table
     marks unknown, one it lacks, a stack of currency — asked by
     `monster.beast`, by each colour, by `currency`, and counted.
   - *Category under `not` and `or`*, worked by hand from the table's
     rows and C93 before running, since the table asks each id as a
     bare term alone: an item whose class is open (the table's
     breachstone and resonator with no frame) and a beast of unknown
     colour, each asked by the not of an id that holds, of an id left
     open, and of an id no candidate gives, and by an or of an open id
     with one that holds. A not of an open id is undecided and never a
     match; a not of an id no candidate gives is a match.
   - *`show`* on a beast, on an item of two leaves, on one with no id
     and on one left open.
   - One CLI `--json` smoke for OQ5 by category and for the beast, and
     `--describe category`.
2. **The reference data and its generator.**
3. **The reading, the term, the count, the help.** Before you write a
   maker, look for the one that exists (rule 10); unknown is said

5. **Three breaks.** Break the build three times, one rule each, your

6. **The copy.** Over the copy as M3 asks it

   `items_listed_under`, each id's term against `items_yes`, the two
   buckets, and what `undecided(class)` counts now. A measurement,
   reported with its command, never a test. The copy is local and may
   be absent: say so and go on.
7. **The gate**, all seven commands, in `AGENTS.md`'s order, after the
   last edit; build again after `cargo fmt` changes a file.
