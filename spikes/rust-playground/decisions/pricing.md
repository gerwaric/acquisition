# Decisions — Pricing: intent values, listing, reference data, plans, import, render

Part of the decision registry (`CONTEXT.md`, "Decisions — the registry"): the rulings that bind the `buyout` intent kind and its target, intent provenance and typed values, the listing state and its inheritance, the currency reference table, `PricePlan` with its receipts and revert, the legacy import, and `shop render` (the pricing module of `acquisition-plan`, `acquisition-store/src/annotations.rs`, the reference table beside its consumer — built by the pricing slice, `PRICING-SLICE.md`, "Plan"). Read this before touching that code; the cross-cutting decisions (C79, governed surfaces, among them) and the invariants are in `CONTEXT.md` and are always loaded. Same rules as there: one entry per decision, stable `C<n>` ids never reused, the ruling verbatim, *Why:*, and pointers; the mechanism lives in the code's doc comments under the id.

Every line here was accepted as written by the owner on 2026-09-03; the lines dated 2026-09-04 were amended after the forum-shop reading (T1–T15, `docs/design/trade-ground-truth.md`; the price-notes run in `LIVE-TESTING.md`) and the owner's re-scoping of the slice to a render-first minimum. Three rulings (C71, C73, C78) keep their ids as direction with their build parked in place, so citations stay valid. The pricing code so far is `acquisition-plan`'s `currency.rs`, `price.rs`, `game_side.rs` and `listing.rs`, the store's `annotations.rs` and the pricing snapshot in `snapshot.rs`, and the CLI's `price_cmd.rs`; an entry's *Details:* names the module doc that carries its mechanism, or the one it will. Under C79 the first registered surface is the trade site, access method `browser`, no automated fetch; the register is `SURFACES.md`. A *Why:* citing `07 §n` or `08 boundary n` names a numbered pattern in `brainstorming-notes/07` or `08` — the deliberation behind the ruling, history rather than a second authority.

## Pricing

- **C64 — Pricing is intent edited offline: manual listing intent is stored as explicit assertions only; game pricing, inheritance and every listing state are derived.** A `buyout` row sits on an item, a tab, a substash or a character; *inherit* is the absence of a row, never a value; a price observed in a tab name or item note is a derivation from a fact, never a row; what an item's listing is — manual side, game side, their relation, its eligibility — is recomputed on read. No pricing operation contacts the daemon, quotes, or creates a job. *Why:* C++ materialized inherited prices onto every item and then needed locks so a refresh could not fight an edit; a derivation cannot be clobbered (C34). *Details:* `acquisition-plan` doc, C64. Ruled 2026-09-03.
- **C65 — Every intent write carries structured provenance: the channel it came through (`written_via`) and an optional claimed `actor`; the hash of the plan that landed it (`applied_plan`, C71) joins when receipts exist.** Stored on the row (annotations v3), returned on every read; `written_via` required by the write API; `actor` is untrusted audit metadata, never identity or authorization; origin detail lives on the receipt (C78). v3 is the floor: an older file is refused, never migrated. *Why:* who set a price and through what cannot be reconstructed later (07 §4), and C14 makes agents writers of intent; cheap today, unrecoverable once rows exist (P3). *Details:* `annotations.rs`. *Pinned:* the `c65_` tests. Ruled 2026-09-03; `applied_plan` deferred 2026-09-04; floor 2026-09-05.
- **C66 — Intent values are typed at the write API: a kind declares its schema version and a strict parser, a value that does not parse under its stamp never lands, and a current-schema value re-serializes to exactly what was read.** The generic (version gate, unknown fields refused, exact round-trip, compare-and-swap) lives in the store crate over a per-kind trait; a kind's shape stays its owner's; an older value upgrades in memory, stored as written. A kind may also refuse as a *new write* what it reads (`check_write`; C67's retired tag first) — at the door, every write, never a read. *Why:* a shape change after data exists migrates the irreplaceable state (07 §4): structural. *Details:* `annotations.rs`. *Pinned:* the `c66_` tests. Ruled 2026-09-03; amended 2026-09-07.
- **C67 — The v1 `buyout` and its target.** A typed `PriceTarget` — item and character by id (C55), tab by `(realm, id)`, substash by `(realm, parent, id)`, league-less — is the public API. `type` is `exact` (`~price`), `negotiable` (`~b/o`), `no_price` or `skip` (`~skip`); the first two carry `amount` and a `currency` tag that must resolve in the reference table (C68). `amount` is a decimal of at most four fractional digits (T10), or a lot ratio `wanted/lot` (T2) of two unreduced positive integers; canonical text; more digits refused. *Why:* the key mirrors the store's identity (C54, C58); the game writes four places (T10), two ruled 2026-09-04; widening is compatible, narrowing is not. *Details:* `price.rs`. *Pinned:* the `c67_` tests. `ignore`→`skip` 2026-09-06, no row existed.
- **C68 — Reference data is a fifth input, a versioned, reviewed, committed table whose rows cite evidence, shipped inside the binary, read-only, never in a store file, enumerable by every surface built, cited by version when used.** A tool may propose rows from a governed source (C79); a human commits. The currency table is first: the immutable `tag` intent cites; `emit`, the word GGG's client writes into a note; `aliases`, the legacy C++ tag and shorthand the owner checked by hand, each cited — the indexer's loose matching is not modelled. A tag is never removed or reused; a dropped currency keeps its row, marked; additions are reported. *Why:* not a fact, not intent, not a derivation (07 §8); the C++ list rotted without evidence. Amended 2026-09-07. *Pinned:* the `c68_` tests.
- **C69 — A listing is 2 independent resolutions, their relation, and the effective price (C81).** The manual side resolves by rules (C70); the game side reads note, then tab name, as a price (exact or negotiable; a ratio too, T2) or `skip` (T10); any other text has no effect (T11, T16, T18, T19), is shown verbatim, and the tab applies. The relation is manual-only, game-only, agree, conflict or none. Each result carries both sides with causes, revisions, basis, versions, the raw texts. *Why:* four statements C++ fused and needed locks; the site's display states are not modelled. *Details:* `game_side.rs`, `listing.rs`. *Pinned:* the `c69_` tests (parse), `c81_` (effective price). Amended 2026-09-06, 2026-09-09.
- **C70 — A priced tab covers that tab and its children, the way a policy id does (C37);** a substash row overrides its parent's for that substash; an item row overrides both; a character row covers its items. Coverage is the manual side's inheritance only — it says nothing about eligibility (C74). *Why:* the C++ store already lands substash items' location prices on the parent, and the house rule for tab-scoped intent should not have two shapes. *Pinned:* the `c70_` tests. Ruled 2026-09-03.
- **C71 — Direction; build parked 2026-09-04. A `PricePlan` compiles a desired state against a named snapshot and is applied atomically to the intent file; its preconditions protect intent, and fact drift is reported, never a gate.** Until the trigger fires, a price is a single-row write through the annotations compare-and-swap, and the one clause needed now — a cited currency tag must resolve at write — lives in C67. Trigger: a batch a human cannot review row by row (an agent writer, or a document of many rows). *Why:* the census found two manual rows; "what was reviewed is what lands" (C38) holds for one row through CAS. *Details:* the mechanism paragraph in `brainstorming-notes/10` until built. Ruled 2026-09-03.
- **C72 — Pricing never edits the sync policy, and a price never locks a tab into refresh.** The relationship between the two kinds of intent is reported, not enforced: the consumer that needs freshness — `shop render` first — names priced locations outside the policy's coverage, priced facts older than its stated window, and items moved or reindexed since the render's basis, each with the remedy (the policy edit, or the `RefreshPlan` it would take, C41). *Why:* C++'s "priced tabs are always refreshed" is one kind of intent silently rewriting another; a report keeps the policy what its author wrote (07 §10, 08 boundary 7). *Details:* `shop.rs`. *Pinned:* the `c72_` test. Ruled 2026-09-03; amended 2026-09-04.
- **C73 — Direction; build parked 2026-09-04. The legacy import is a source of desired state for the one compiler (C71), read from the 0.18 userstore under one consistent snapshot, with every source row accounted for in the reviewed plan.** Manual rows are the desired state; inherited, game, auto and `~c/o` rows are non-actions; an existing row is never overwritten; the realm is explicit; the file's username binds to a uuid with stated evidence, only contradiction refusing. The owner's file held two manual rows (census, 2c), ruled residue and dropped 2026-09-06; nothing is carried. Trigger: a 0.18 user with manual rows asks for them carried over. *Why:* the wizard dissolves into the plan/apply path; two rows do not justify building it first. Ruled 2026-09-03.
- **C74 — `shop render` is in scope as a derivation, publishing isn't, and every item the page omits is counted with a named reason.** The page is a pure function of facts, intent, reference data, a template and a ruled policy; it is written to stdout in pages labelled *n* of *N*; it sends nothing; the template is an input, not stored. Nothing is skipped silently: what the game already lists (C81) is omitted and counted, an unruled cell is blocked, a prior post is never read (T6). The policy's rows cite trade claims (T7, T13) and owner observations (T11, T12), never its own output. *Why:* every human surface is a derivation over a machine surface (07 §3); posting stays parked behind its own session. *Details:* `shop.rs`. *Pinned:* the `c74_` tests. Amended 2026-09-06.
- **C75 — Pricing lives in `acquisition-plan` as a module; `PricePlan` is built operation-specific, and what the two plan-bearing consumers genuinely share is recorded after it exists, not presumed.** The hypothesis to test: the shared thing is the compile → review → apply loop and an envelope discipline (stamp, strict round-trip parse, provider and uuid, basis, precondition set, non-actions, counts), and not the action vocabulary or the apply target. The test waits for the second consumer (C71, parked 2026-09-04). A crate split waits for a dependency-graph property it would buy. *Why:* generalize after two consumers reveal the shared property (P3); ruling "never a grammar" up front would prejudge the test. Ruled 2026-09-03.
- **C78 — Direction; build parked 2026-09-04 with C71. Every applied price plan leaves an intent receipt in the same transaction, from which a conditional inverse can be compiled.** A receipt (annotations v3) holds the plan's hash, when and through what it was applied, its origin, its counts, and each mutation with prior and written values; `revert` compiles a new plan against current revisions and refuses whole if any row moved — history is evidence, never replayed. Until then a single write returns the row's prior value, so it can be undone by hand. Trigger: C71's. *Why:* the driver must answer "did that land?" and "undo it" for a batch of hundreds; no batch exists yet. *Details:* `annotations.rs` doc, when built. *Pinned:* the `c78_` test. Ruled 2026-09-03.
- **C81 — The effective price is the most specific statement, the game's on a tie; only a public tab's game side is a statement.** Levels are C70's; the game speaks at item level (a note) and tab level (a name, C80). A note or name in a non-public tab is invisible to the index (T1, T11, T25) — residue, shown, never a side. A prior forum post is never a side (T6). A row that cannot be read, at a level that could decide, leaves the price unresolved — never the game's by default. *Why:* acquisition's prices reach the world only through the forum, and a page must not contradict what the site already shows; owner, 2026-09-06: "In-game prices should take priority over acquisition prices." *Details:* `listing.rs`. *Pinned:* the `c81_` tests. Ruled 2026-09-06.
- **C80 — The game side's tab for a nested item is the tab a user can name and publish: a substash reads its parent, a folder child reads itself, a folder's name is never a price.** A map or unique substash has the game's own name and no `public`, so an item in one reads its note, then the parent's name and `public`, the substash's name reported verbatim beside it. A folder is a grouping, not a tab: its child's own name and `public` are read. Confirmed on the site 2026-09-09, all three ways (`PRICING-SLICE.md`, "What the site taught", 12). *Why:* the owner's facts hold 64 substashes with no settable name or `public` and 77 folder children as ordinary tabs; owner, 2026-09-06: "Folders never carry price, but substash parents can." *Pinned:* the `c80_` tests. Ruled 2026-09-06.

## Parked (do not build yet; each with its trigger)

Scope this area has deferred, with the trigger that reopens it, so deferral never needs re-arguing. A fired trigger deletes its entry in the build's commit; what crosses every area is parked in `CONTEXT.md` instead. An entry is the item, where it lands, and the trigger that reopens it, with at most one clause of why; a workaround is a README known gap, history is git. C71, C73 and C78 are parked in place above, under their ids.

- Third-party price feeds (market prices, suggested prices) → a governed surface under C79. Trigger: currency ratios or a "suggest a price" consumer.
- The bulk-exchange item list (which items the trade site groups under a ratio price) → a second reference table under C68. The list itself is read: `reference/exchange-items-2026-09-08.json`, 750 ids in 15 of the site's 22 groups, from the owner's saved exchange page (2026-09-08; the seven unexpanded groups named in it). Trigger for the table and its reader: the owner prices a non-currency item by ratio, or a manual row lands on a stack in a ratio-named public tab (`PRICING-SLICE.md`, "What the site taught", 2).
- Row-granularity annotation history → the intent file. Trigger: a "since" question at the pricing readings that a write's returned prior value cannot answer, or a conflict whose resolution needs more than two writes' history.
- One change cursor over facts and intent (`item_events` and intent revisions read as one "since" stream) → the store's read API. Trigger: an agent consumer reading facts and intent together shows two reads where one would do.
- Batch pricing by query (price everything a search selects) → search semantics. Trigger: a real batch workflow the handles cannot express.
- Current offer (`~c/o`) as a value → the `buyout` value (C67). Trigger: a real use; the census found zero rows.
- PoE2 currencies and per-realm tags → rows in the currency table, whose shape carries realm applicability from day one. Trigger: a PoE2 stash endpoint, or a poe2 price the owner wants to set.
- Currency totals and history → a derivation over facts. Trigger: the owner asks for it (2026-09-09: "this will be a \"when I asked for it\" situation").
- Name→id resolution for price targets (`price set tab:"Maps"`) → the CLI, the same rule as C63. Trigger: authoring friction.
- "What did I last post" (a stored render basis, a changed-since line) → user-scoped intent (`decisions/store.md`). Trigger: a user asks; v1 reposts whole pages, since the forum resolves links at post time and reports nothing back.
